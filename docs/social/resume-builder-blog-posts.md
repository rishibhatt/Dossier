## 1. free-resume-builder-no-watermark

**LinkedIn**

A lot of "free" resume builders follow one pattern: the editor is free, and the download asks for a card.

Before you type a line into any builder, open its pricing page and search for the word "download". "Free to create" and "free to download" are different promises.

Then check three more things. What format does the free plan export? Is there a watermark or "Made with" footer? Does a cheap trial renew monthly?

I build Dossier Resume, so I am biased. Our PDF download is free on every template, with no watermark.

## 2. ats-friendly-resume-template

**LinkedIn**

Almost every resume template online says "ATS-friendly". Here is a two-minute test you can run on any of them.

Open the PDF and select one line. If it highlights word by word, the text is real. Then copy the whole page into a plain text editor. The lines should come out in the order you would read them. If your job title lands next to a skill from the sidebar, a parser will read it that way too.

Fonts and colour barely matter. Reading order does.

We test our own templates by reading each PDF back with a parser and publish the dated results on every template page. The other five checks are in the guide, link in comments.

**X**

The 2-minute ATS test for any resume template:

1. Open the PDF, select a line. Real text?
2. Copy the page into a plain text editor. Same order you read it?

If either fails, the layout is the problem, not your keywords.

## 3. how-long-should-a-resume-be

**LinkedIn**

"One page" is US advice that travels badly.

A UK CV is normally two pages. Germany expects one to two. Gulf employers often see two to three, because nationality, visa status and notice period go at the top. Academic CVs have no limit at all.

The rule that does hold everywhere: if page two has four lines on it, cut until it is gone. A half-empty page looks like an accident.

I wrote up the length by career stage and by country, plus what to cut first when you run over. Link in comments.

## 4. career-change-resume

**LinkedIn**

A recruiter reads your job titles before your bullets. If every title says Teacher and you are applying for Instructional Designer, they decide before they reach the proof.

So move the proof up. Summary and skills at the top, then your history with dates, newest first. Avoid the functional format that hides dates. Recruiters read it as a sign something is missing.

Then rewrite old bullets in the new field's words, where it is honest. "Planned lessons for Year 9 science" becomes "Designed 30 lesson units with assessments and learning objectives for 120 students."

Full guide with examples for teachers, retail managers and nurses in the comments.

## 5. employment-gap-on-resume

**LinkedIn**

A gap on a resume only hurts when the reader has to guess what happened.

Under six months, say nothing. Over six months, one plain line in the experience section with its own dates: "Career break, family care, Mar 2023 to Jan 2025." No apology, no diagnosis, no invented consulting work.

In the interview, two sentences: what happened, and why you are ready now. Then talk about the job.

Example wording for parental leave, health, relocation and layoffs is in the guide. Link in comments.

## 6. german-cv-format and cv-format-for-gulf-jobs (pair)

**LinkedIn**

The same resume can be wrong in three countries at once.

In the UK, a photo and date of birth are left out. In Germany, many applicants still include both, and every language needs a level such as B2, not "good". In the UAE, recruiters want nationality, visa status and notice period near the top, because those decide how fast you can start.

Dossier Resume switches paper size, date style and personal fields when you pick a country. I also wrote a guide for each. Links in comments.

## 7. resume-format-for-freshers

**LinkedIn**

For a first resume, the order matters more than the template.

Education, then projects, then any work at all, including part-time jobs. Write each project like a job: what you built, with what, and what came of it. "Python, Pandas" in a skills list says less than one bullet about a script the placement cell actually used.

Two sample fresher resumes and the full section-by-section guide are linked in the comments. The builder and PDF download are free.
